# Contributing to Dr. Dolittle

Thank you for considering a contribution. This is an early-stage, open comparative research initiative. Contributions from ethology, behavioral ecology, bioacoustics, linguistics, semiotics, evolutionary biology, data science, computer vision, and interested independent researchers are welcome.

## Ways to contribute

- Review definitions and propose clearer operational tests.
- Add peer-reviewed evidence or counterevidence with precise citations.
- Identify understudied modalities and animal groups.
- Suggest reproducible datasets, with licensing and provenance.
- Improve accessibility, diagrams, documentation, or methodological rigor.
- Propose small computational experiments with baselines and falsifiable outcomes.

## Before submitting

1. Search existing issues and documentation for related work.
2. For substantive changes, open an issue stating the research question, evidence, and expected change.
3. Keep pull requests small and focused. Explain assumptions and alternatives.
4. Cite primary sources where possible, including DOI/URL and relevant limitations.
5. Clearly label **observation**, **inference**, **hypothesis**, and **unknown**.
6. Do not upload copyrighted recordings, personal data, or restricted datasets without permission.

## Evidence quality

Do not present a model-generated label as an observed animal state. Do not equate signal clustering with semantics. Do not treat missing research as evidence of absence. Avoid anthropomorphic or evolutionary-progress narratives. A claim of convergence needs comparison against phylogenetic alternatives.

## Research ethics

Prefer non-invasive existing data. Any future collection or playback experiment involving animals must comply with applicable welfare rules, permits, and institutional review requirements. No such experiment is currently authorized by this repository.

## Review and governance

The repository maintainers review changes. A merged hypothesis is not a validated finding. Substantive claims should have traceable sources and explicit confidence. Respectful disagreement and negative findings are welcome.
