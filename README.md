# Dr. Dolittle
### A Comparative Atlas of Animal Communication

**How does communication evolve across the animal kingdom — and which capabilities do very different species share?**

Dr. Dolittle is an **open, early-stage research initiative** to map the diversity of animal communication. Rather than attempting to translate animal sounds into human words, we begin with a more fundamental question: **what constitutes a communication system, how is it structured, and how can its capabilities be compared across evolutionary lineages?**

Our working approach is *phenomenon-first*: classify general communication mechanisms and capabilities, compare broad animal groups, and only then select individual species for detailed study. Humans provide a richly documented comparative reference, **not** an evolutionary endpoint.

> **Research status: Proposed / foundational documentation.** This repository currently contains a conceptual framework and research plan. It does **not** claim a validated atlas, trained model, completed dataset audit, animal-language translation, or published experimental findings.

## Why this matters

Animal communication extends far beyond vocalizations. It can involve gesture, posture, chemical cues, touch, vibration, electrical signals, and multimodal combinations. Some systems are highly specialized; others exhibit contextual flexibility, learning, or complex social organization. Similar capabilities can arise in distant lineages — but superficial resemblance does not prove common meaning or ancestry.

By comparing **capabilities rather than ranking species**, we hope to identify conserved patterns, convergent innovations, and informative gaps in the evidence. The longer-term opportunity is to make computational discovery more interpretable, testable, and cumulative.

## Research at a glance

| We investigate | We do not assume |
| --- | --- |
| Signal modalities and their physical constraints | All communication is vocal |
| Structure, sequences, and combinations | A sound cluster is a word |
| Context, receivers, and behavioral responses | Correlation establishes intention |
| Flexibility, learning, and social transmission | Human language is the universal template |
| Comparative patterns and evolutionary hypotheses | Similarity alone proves shared ancestry |
| Evidence quality and uncertainty | Lack of studies means lack of capability |

## Our research strategy

**01 · Define communication phenomena** → distinguish signals, cues, observable responses, and interpretations.

**02 · Classify general capabilities** → map modality, signal structure, function, flexibility, social organization, and informational properties as separate axes.

**03 · Compare animal groups** → survey broad groups such as social insects, birds, fishes, mammals, and cephalopods, without treating them as rungs on an evolutionary ladder.

**04 · Narrow to species and evidence** → choose representative species and datasets only after identifying useful comparisons and research gaps.

**05 · Test hypotheses** → where justified, use reproducible multimodal analyses of audio, movement, behavior, and context, with independent validation.

Interspecies communication may provide **supporting evidence** of shared perceptual or communicative capacities, but a comprehensive interspecies survey is outside the initial scope.

## Where collaboration can make a difference

We welcome contributions from **ethologists, bioacousticians, evolutionary biologists, linguists, semioticians, animal behavior researchers, machine-learning practitioners, and curious independent contributors**.

Especially valuable early contributions include rigorous definitions, literature-backed comparisons, negative evidence, overlooked communication modalities, and dataset evaluations. **You do not need to train an AI model to contribute meaningfully.**

## Scientific commitments

- **Evidence before interpretation.** Separate observed phenomena from inferred functions, emotions, intentions, and meanings.
- **Uncertainty is data.** Distinguish documented presence, demonstrated absence under defined tests, conflicting evidence, and unknowns.
- **Evolution is not a ladder.** Compare capabilities while accounting for phylogeny, ecology, and convergence.
- **Reproducibility matters.** Record provenance, limitations, licensing, controls, and evaluation procedures.
- **Animal welfare matters.** Prefer ethical, non-invasive data and appropriate approvals for any future experiments.
- **Open collaboration, careful claims.** A public proposal is not an established result.
