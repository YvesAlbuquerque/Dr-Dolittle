# R1a — Competing definitions of animal communication: critical comparison (v0.1)

**Research date:** 2026-11-23  
**Status:** preliminary source-based **theoretical synthesis**, not a completed scoping review, independent expert review, or accepted empirical evidence.  
**Scope:** definitions of *signal*, *cue*, *communication*, *information*, *influence*, *meaning*. No species-level capability claims are established by this document.

## Epistemic discipline

- **Full online text inspected** means the accessible publisher HTML was inspected for the named argument, **not** that every reference, experimental example, or empirical conclusion has been independently checked.
- **Abstract/repository record inspected** means the argument below is restricted to the accessible abstract, introduction, or bibliographic record.
- **Indirect** means quoted or summarized through another author; do not misrepresent this as reading the original book.
- We compare mutually incompatible *theoretical* positions; their disagreement is not a data error to be averaged away.

### What the sources actually agree on

1. Signals must be analyzed with reference to **receivers**, not inferred from emitter behavior alone.
2. A measurable change in receiver behavior is not enough to establish evolved function, subjective intention, or semantics.
3. Observable pattern, function and interpretation should be different data fields.
4. Context and alternative explanations matter. Apparent information transfer, influence and adaptation cannot be assumed interchangeable.

These are a **synthesis for project design**, not a claim that all the authors adopt exactly the same terminology.

### Unresolved disagreements

- **Definition:** is evidence of evolutionary adaptation necessary to label an interaction communication, or can learned/developmental signaling be described more broadly?
- **Information:** is it a causal/receiver-specific explanatory tool, a functional correspondence, or a misleading metaphor when used without operational tests?
- **Signal vs cue:** when do persistent incidental correlates become signals through selection or learned usage?
- **Meaning:** does describing functional consequences require a separate semantic category, and how could one test it without attributing human intention?
- **Unit of analysis:** are networks, multiple receivers, unintended audiences and sequential feedback essential parts of the event model?

## Our provisional operational policy — NOT a resolution of the debate

**Tier 0 — Observable event:** an explicitly described behavioral, physiological or environmental change with timestamps, individuals, context and detectable modalities, irrespective of function.

**Tier 1 — Receiver-associated cue / interaction:** evidence that another organism detects or responds to that change; no adaptation or intentionality assumed.

**Tier 2 — Candidate communicative signal:** experimental, developmental or evolutionary evidence that the producer's action and receiver response are organized for an effect in an interaction. State which theoretical account supports this classification.

**Tier 3 — Stronger functional or semantic hypothesis:** requires independent tests of contingency, signal specificity, receptor state, alternative explanations and, for strong claims, interventions/playback or comparative/phylogenetic support. Do not equate with speech.

These are **review/interpretation tiers**, not a universal biological hierarchy. A phenomenon can carry multiple competing framework-specific interpretations and confidence judgments.

## Stress test: a deep breath is not already a signal

A conspicuous breath can be recorded at Tier 0; a companion responding to it may yield Tier 1; calling it communicative at Tier 2 requires additional producer/receiver functional evidence. See [respiratory phenomena research note](respiration-and-communication.md) for competing hypotheses and selected primary studies.
