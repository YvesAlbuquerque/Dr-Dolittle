# R1 — Communication: working definitions and classification protocol

**Status: Proposed.** This document is a discussion-ready starting point, not a systematic literature review or validated ontology.

## Why start here?

Before selecting species or building AI models, we need reproducible criteria for what counts as communication and which properties can be compared. A sound, posture, scent, or electrical discharge can carry information without necessarily functioning as a communicative signal.

## Provisional distinctions

| Concept | Working interpretation | Main empirical question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information-bearing cue | An observable feature from which a receiver may obtain information, without presuming selection for signaling | Does the receiver respond, and what alternative mechanisms explain the response? |
| Communicative signal | A trait or act whose production and receiver response have been shaped by communicative functions, in an evolutionary or learned context | What evidence supports production-for-effect and reception mechanisms? |
| Communicative event | A situated episode involving a producer, signal/cue, potential receiver(s), context, and observable outcome | What exactly was observed and measured? |
| Communication system | Recurrent patterns linking signal production, perception, context, and consequences | Are patterns stable, variable, learned, or context-dependent? |
| Capability | An operationally defined property that can recur across systems and lineages | Can it be tested independently of modality and species? |

These definitions need comparison against competing frameworks; no single definition should be presented as universal consensus.

## Proposed first-pass classification

**Physical channel:** acoustic, visual, chemical, tactile, substrate-borne vibrational, electrical, or combined.

**Signal organization:** discrete/graded, temporal patterning, sequentiality, combination, spatial organization, persistence.

**Interaction:** one-to-one, broadcast, group-mediated, distributed; receiver identity, audience effects, feedback.

**Behavioral function:** observed changes in attention, movement, coordination, mating, defense, affiliation, or resource acquisition. Function must be distinguished from inferred meaning.

**Plasticity:** developmental changes, individual learning, socially mediated learning, context sensitivity.

**Inferential/cognitive claims:** reference-like behavior, intentionality, compositionality, or semantics. These require stronger evidence than signal classification alone.

## Classification procedure

1. Identify the smallest well-described communicative phenomenon from a primary study.
2. Record the actual observations, study design, group, context, and modality.
3. Code independent axes separately; allow multiple labels and uncertainty.
4. Record evidence as documented presence, documented absence under a defined test, conflicting, insufficient, not assessed, or hypothesis only.
5. Note alternative explanations and methodological limitations.
6. Aggregate cautiously to animal groups, preserving within-group variation.
7. Select candidate species only after identifying an informative comparison.

## Questions for collaborators

1. Is the signal/cue distinction adequate for learned and domesticated interactions?
2. How should we represent receiver-centered phenomena and unintended audiences?
3. Should communication systems be indexed by *interaction* rather than by producer species?
4. Which categories are empirically operationalizable without importing human-language assumptions?
5. What would constitute defensible evidence of absence at the group level?

## R1 acceptance criteria

- A cited review of competing communication definitions.
- A versioned glossary with operational criteria.
- At least two independent reviewers' critiques or documented competing perspectives.
- Pilot coding of contrasting examples across different modalities, with disagreement recorded.
- No claim of a completed cross-species atlas before these checks.
