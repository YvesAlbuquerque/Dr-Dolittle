# Methodology: phenomena before species

**Status: Proposed.** The workflow is a research plan, not an executed protocol.

## Stage 1 — establish definitions

Review comparative communication theory, biosemiotics, ethology, behavioral ecology, language evolution, and computational bioacoustics. Explicitly distinguish signals, cues, responses, and semantic hypotheses. Register disagreements rather than forcing consensus.

## Stage 2 — build broad communication classes

Develop independent axes for modality, structure, function, flexibility, social organization, and informational capacities. Define inclusion/exclusion criteria and measurable observables. Avoid imposing human linguistic categories on all animals.

### Physiological and molecular evidence — supplemental axis

Record physiology relevant to communication **at any stage** when supported by a cited study: endocrine regulation, neural production/perception mechanisms, autonomic/cardiorespiratory measurements, and conserved ligand/receptor systems. These are study measurements, not a new sensory modality or proof of external signaling. Preserve biological matrix, assay, relative timing, within-animal baseline, potential receiver and confounds. Separate hormone homology from conservation of function and keep biological evolution distinct from cross-taxon behavioral analogy. See [dedicated protocol](physiological-biomarkers-and-communication.md).

## Stage 3 — survey animal groups

Build an evidence matrix at the **group** level first. Each claim should point to specific studies and carry a status reflecting evidence quality and uncertainty. Group summaries must not imply every species in the group has a trait.

## Stage 4 — identify informative comparisons

Select comparisons based on potential convergence, divergence, missing evidence, and complementary modalities. Overlay known phylogenies when making evolutionary claims; similarity maps alone cannot reconstruct ancestry.

## Stage 5 — choose species and datasets

Only then choose representative species. Audit data licenses, sample size, recording frequency and bandwidth, synchronization, individual identities, behavior labels, annotation quality, and population/context diversity. Avoid treating public video quantity as equivalent to research-grade synchronized observations.

## Stage 6 — validate computational hypotheses

Possible future methods include acoustic embeddings, pose estimation, multimodal contrastive learning, temporal prediction, and clustering. Pre-register baselines and splits by individual, site, session, and context when feasible. Test whether audio improves future-behavior prediction beyond video alone. Include negative controls and uncertainty calibration.

A statistically coherent cluster is **not** evidence of emotion, intention, wordhood, or translation. Functional interpretations require independent behavioral tests; stronger semantic claims may require controlled playback or intervention experiments with appropriate ethics review.

## Evidence governance

- Link primary sources; mark secondary summaries.
- Separate facts, interpretations, hypotheses, and open questions.
- Report contradictory and negative findings.
- Record dataset licensing, provenance, and any ethical restrictions.
- Require human review before promoting claims to established evidence.
- Never describe planned analyses as completed results.

## Interspecies bridges

Cross-species interpretation may serve as *supporting evidence* for a shared perceptual or communicative capacity. It does not automatically imply a shared language or common evolutionary origin and is outside the initial systematic survey.
